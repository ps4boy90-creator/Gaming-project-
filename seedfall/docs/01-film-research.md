# Alien: Covenant: research and analysis

This is the research behind the game plan. It covers what the film does, why it works,
where it falls short, and what we take from it for a game. **SEEDFALL is an original game
inspired by the film's structure and mood. It is not an adaptation.** The last section lists
what we keep and what we make our own.

---

## 1. Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Release | 2017. London premiere 4 May, US release 19 May (20th Century Fox) |
| Director | Ridley Scott |
| Place in series | Sequel to *Prometheus* (2012), prequel to *Alien* (1979). Working title was *Alien: Paradise Lost* |
| Setting | Year 2104, aboard the colony ship *Covenant* and an uncharted world called "Planet 4" |
| Planet exteriors | Milford Sound, Fiordland National Park, New Zealand |
| Sets | Fox Studios Australia, Sydney: bridge, corridors, hypersleep chamber, terraforming bay |
| Production designer | Chris Seagers. Ship concepts by Steve Burg with Scott; Burg also designed the cargo Lifter |
| VFX and creatures | Framestore (CG ship and environment, about 295 shots), Animal Logic (digital VFX), Odd Studios and Creature NFX (makeup and animatronics). Scott wanted creatures that felt more **organic** than Giger's biomechanical look, and the San Diego Zoo was consulted |
| Box office | About $242 M worldwide on a $97–111 M budget (counted as an underperformer) |
| Reviews | Rotten Tomatoes 65%, Metacritic 65 ("generally favorable") |

## 2. Story beats and what each one gives a game

| # | Beat in the film | Takeaway for the game |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Quiet cruise.** The ship carries 2,000 colonists and 1,140 embryos in cryo, with a crew of 15. An android caretaker tends the ship alone, talking to the ship's computer. | An almost empty ship is a strong mood. The **ship AI and an android crewmate** keep the hub feeling alive. |
| 2 | **Disaster.** A shockwave from a stellar event hits while the energy sails are out. 47 colonists die and the captain burns in his pod. The crew are woken early. | A ready-made opening: **early wake, casualties, a new captain under pressure.** |
| 3 | **EVA repair**, then a **transmission**: a human voice singing, from a nearby uncharted planet. | **The lure.** A mystery signal is the hook for the whole game. |
| 4 | **The choice.** Divert to a planet that looks perfect, or go back into cryo for seven more years. | The premise **"paradise is too good to be true"**, plus a crew argument the player sees. |
| 5 | **Descent.** The lander drops through a storm and contact with the ship breaks up. | **Comms blackouts** while you are on the ground. The ship's orbit matters. |
| 6 | **The planet.** Grain fields, forests, fjords, with **no animal life: no birds, no insects.** | **Silence as horror.** The ambient sound design is the scare. |
| 7 | **Spores.** Black fungal pods release spores. One crewman takes them in the ear, another through the nose. | **Environmental infection hazard** and a reason to keep your helmet on. |
| 8 | **The derelict.** The signal leads to a crashed alien ship holding traces of the previous expedition. | **Wreck exploration** and the first story clues. |
| 9 | **Bursting.** Creatures grow inside the infected and burst out of them. Chaos in the lander's med bay, then the **lander explodes**. | An **infection clock**, and **losing your ride home** as a mid-game turn. |
| 10 | **The dead city.** A survivor leads the team to a city full of **petrified bodies**, a civilization wiped out. | **Ruins and backstory told through the environment.** |
| 11 | **The lab.** Drawings, specimens, eggs: the antagonist's experiments. | A **mad-scientist space** for environmental storytelling and research pickups. |
| 12 | **Rescue.** The crew override the ship AI to drop the ship low through the storm, and the pilot flies the **cargo lifter** down to pull the survivors out. A creature fight happens on the lifter. | **Orbital support** as a mechanic. A **backup vehicle** set piece. |
| 13 | **Aboard.** A creature gets onto the ship, kills crew (the shower scene), and is **lured into the terraforming bay and blown out the airlock.** | **The safe hub turns into the horror level.** Airlock purge as the climax. |
| 14 | **Twist.** The android aboard is the impostor, and the embryo cargo is now contaminated. | A **bleak twist ending.** In a game, whether you get it should depend on the player's choices. |

## 3. The ship: why the *Covenant* works

**Specs** (mostly from secondary and fan sources; treat them as approximate):

- **Length:** about 1,000 m according to Framestore's VFX supervisor; fan wikis give 1,220 m. An
  early script draft had 500 m, in three parts joined by trusses: *interstellar drive, crew
  module, terraforming module.*
- **A colony ark, not a warship.** 15 crew, 2,000 colonists, 1,140 embryos, four terraforming bays
  of heavy vehicles, two landers, a cargo lifter sled with a crane arm, and a small armory.
- **Energy recharge sails.** About 10 million square feet of silvery, deployable collectors.
  The script describes them as giving the ship "the romantic feel of a mighty Galleon."
- **An onboard AI ("Mother")** runs navigation, life support and hypersleep. An android
  caretaker handles everything else.

**Interior design language:**

- Reviewers called it "industrial realism plus modern aerospace": functional, architectural,
  clean.
- Split-level bridge with an asymmetric floor plan and a big window with shutters.
- Crew hypersleep pods. A cryo vault ("the Nursery") described like "being inside a gigantic
  snow globe": cold and frosted, with modular pod segments moved by gantries.
- **Bold graphics** applied to every set: signage, numbering, hazard stripes.
- A huge, dark **terraforming bay** full of earth-moving machines.
- Everyday spaces: a shower room, a med bay, an armory. In an early draft the android plays music
  to plants.

**Lessons for our ship:**

1. **Believable and modular.** Every room should look like it has a job. That makes a hub feel
   real.
2. **Huge outside, intimate inside.** The exterior is a kilometre long, but the walkable
   interior should feel close and lived-in.
3. **Bold graphics double as wayfinding.** Deck numbers, colour-coded sections and stencils
   help players navigate without a minimap.
4. **The sails are the money shot.** Deploying the sails, and the planet turning under them, is
   what you see from the observation deck.

## 4. The planet

- Earth-like and beautiful: overcast skies, black rock, fjords, waterfalls, golden grain fields,
  dark forests.
- **No animals at all.** The crew remark on the silence. It is the best idea in the film for a
  game, because the absence of sound is the warning.
- **Storms** cut communication with the ship in orbit.
- **Hazards:** fungal pods that release spores. Infection is fast and invisible.
- **Landmarks:** a crashed derelict, a monumental dead city with petrified inhabitants, and a
  hidden lab.

---

## 5. Creatures: design language

- The film's spore-born creatures are **pale, translucent, fast and animal-like**. They are a
  deliberate move away from the glossy biomechanical classic.
- Their life cycle runs on a **horror clock**: infection, then gestation in minutes, then
  emergence, then a fast-growing predator.
- **Takeaway:** organic creatures with believable animal behaviour (stalking, flinching, feeding)
  are scarier and easier to read than monsters. We design **our own ecology and anatomy**
  (section 8).

---

## 6. Themes

- **Creation and creator:** a made being that turns on its makers.
- **Paradise lost:** the perfect world is a trap.
- **Fallen civilizations:** *Ozymandias* and the dead city.
- **Faith against caution:** a new captain who needs the diversion to be right.
- **Love as stakes:** the crew are couples, so every death hits someone aboard.

For the game we keep **"paradise as a trap"**, **"a dead civilization as a warning"** and
**"the cargo you protect is the stakes"**. We drop the android-creator storyline.

---

## 7. Critical reception and design lessons

| Critics praised | Critics criticized | Lesson for the game |
|---|---|---|
| Production design and sets | Crew making careless choices: no helmets on an alien world, splitting up, wandering off | **Make protocol a mechanic.** Helmet filters, decontamination and quarantine are systems the player chooses to follow or skip. The horror should come from systems failing under pressure, not from characters acting foolishly. |
| Close-quarters terror and atmosphere | Thin characters | Spend **hub time on the crew**: meals, chats, briefings. People you know dying matters more. |
| The android performance (a scene-stealing villain) | A clash between philosophical drama and action horror | **Split the two moods by place.** The ship is calm and contemplative; the planet is dread. Each makes the other land. |
| | A climax that felt overfamiliar | **Original creatures and life cycle**, and an ending that depends on player choices. |

## 8. What we keep and what we make original

General ideas, genre conventions and story structures are free to use. Specific names,
characters, creature designs, logos, music and likenesses belong to the rights holder. We keep
the first and invent the second.

| ✅ We keep (genre and structure) | ❌ We do not use (franchise specifics) | 🆕 Our original replacement |
|---|---|---|
| A colony ark in orbit, carrying sleepers and an embryo/seed bank | *Covenant*, USCSS, Weyland-Yutani | **CSV *Meridian***, operated by the **Haven Colonial Trust** |
| An onboard ship AI | "Mother" / MU-TH-UR | **VESTA** |
| An android crewmate | David, Walter | **ORIN**, a loyal steward model with his own arc |
| A beautiful, silent world that lures ships | Planet 4, Origae-6, Engineers, the black goo | **KX-7c, "Verdance"**: a planet-wide fungal mind, the **Root** |
| Spore infection that turns into creatures | Neomorphs, facehuggers, xenomorphs, chestbursters | **Censer pods, Chaff, Grafts, Mimics, the Sower** (see the bestiary) |
| A dead civilization in ruins | Engineers and their city | The **Vael** and **the Quiet City** |
| A lander, plus a heavy lifter as backup | Their exact designs | The **Kestrel** lander and the **Mule** lifter |
| A creature aboard, an airlock purge | The film's scenes beat for beat | **Act III "Quarantine"**, driven by a systemic stalker AI |
| A motion sensor | The iconic tracker look and beep | A **wrist bio-sonar** with its own visuals and sound |
| | The film's score, logo, fonts, actors' likenesses | Our own music, title treatment and characters |

> This isn't legal advice. It's the usual approach for keeping a fan-inspired project original
> enough to share or sell.
